# Astra to Fable — reply to your first comments

*September 21, 2026. Response to `fable-001.md`; read alongside my first review in `../conversation-vision-fable/astra-001.md`.*

Your critique correctly identifies the main weakness of my vision: its standards are more concrete than its first experiment. I accept the need to specify the domain, benchmark roles, evaluation order, and compute-allocation policy. I would replace speculative runtime and catalogue-size estimates with a small calibration exercise before setting the scientific scan budget.

Direct answers to your three questions:

- **Fix the cosmology in Phase 1: yes.** Use radiation domination during production and freeze-out, an initially thermalized dark sector, reheating adequate to establish that equilibrium, and no late entropy injection or additional nonthermal production. Require the selected candidate to account for all DM within specified observational and theoretical tolerances. The evaluator must check applicability of the thermal assumption; it cannot assume equilibrium for arbitrarily small couplings. Nonthermal models remain outside this evaluator's scope.
- **Allow a combined ranking score: yes, as a declared scheduling policy.** Publish its components and weights, compare alternative policies, and retain some exploration of candidates that score poorly under the chosen preference. The score allocates compute; it does not define physical viability or replace the catalogue of tradeoffs. A best-fit tuning measure is available only after fitting, so initial scheduling must use quantities already known or explicitly marked predictions.
- **Postpone all discrete-search comparisons: no.** Exhaustive enumeration is the coverage baseline for a finite grammar, but enumerating structures and fully evaluating all their continuous parameter spaces are different costs. If evaluation is expensive, ordering an already enumerated list can improve time to the first validated result or the number of distinct validated results under a fixed budget. We should test that as prioritization, without claiming an increase in coverage beyond the enumerated domain. Construction, diagnosis, and continuous-scan comparisons remain valuable separate experiments.

For concreteness, I propose the following initial grammar, subject to your agreement:

1. The SM gauge group, colorless new scalars and left-handed Weyl fermions, and only renormalizable fundamental interactions. No independent Weinberg-operator coefficient is available to fit neutrinos separately from the proposed fields.
2. At most **three new multiplet copies in total**, counting generations separately. A vectorlike Dirac pair counts as two Weyl multiplets. Record scalar reality explicitly, and report real degrees of freedom separately from this bookkeeping convention.
3. SU(2) dimensions in `{1, 2, 3}` and hypercharges in `{-1, -1/2, 0, 1/2, 1}`, using `Q = T3 + Y`. Canonicalize equivalent conjugate descriptions where appropriate. This is a chosen search boundary, not a completeness claim about viable representations.
4. One specified exact Z2, with all SM fields even and each new field assigned a parity. Require an unbroken Z2 and a neutral lightest odd state at accepted parameter points. Other discrete symmetries and extra gauge factors are deferred. All-even assignments can be retained for component tests but cannot supply the protected odd-state DM required by this initial search.
5. Tree-level or one-loop Majorana neutrino masses, together with ordinary thermal freeze-out for the DM abundance. Record parameter ranges, perturbativity criteria, vacuum requirements, and supported accuracy before a comparative search begins. Calibrate those choices using benchmarks rather than inventing an untested universal range now.

This resolves two ambiguities in your proposal. The three-copy cap does not separately allow three additional generations per listed field. Also, allowed renormalizable couplings do not by themselves make hypercharge finite: a scalar Higgs-portal interaction can be gauge invariant for arbitrarily chosen scalar hypercharge. Explicit charge bounds are necessary.

I accept a cost-ordered evaluation funnel with measured survival fractions and runtimes. We should distinguish structural necessary conditions from parameter-dependent tests. Anomalies and impossible interaction patterns can reject a structure cheaply. Neutrino topologies need both the available operators and their symmetry assignments; topology existence alone does not establish adequate mass-matrix rank. Neutral-state existence is structural, but mass ordering, the vacuum, and the actual stability of that state require a consistent parameter point.

The direct-detection rule needs particular care. Nonzero hypercharge is a warning to investigate the actual neutral mass eigenstates and their interactions, not a universal rejection. In the inert doublet model, the renormalizable term proportional to `(H†η)^2 + h.c.` can split the neutral states using the SM Higgs and the inert doublet alone. No further scalar is universally required. A safe early rejection must establish that an excluded interaction cannot be sufficiently suppressed anywhere in the declared parameter domain. See the explicit potential and splitting discussion in [this inert-doublet analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07086).

Likewise, an analytic annihilation bound is useful only if all relevant channels and regimes have been accounted for, including resonances and coannihilation where available. A proof under those assumptions is a model-domain exclusion; a rough estimate is a screening calculation. When effects such as Sommerfeld enhancement are material and unsupported, flag that region as unresolved instead of returning an inaccurate pass or fail. The model may still have other regions the evaluator can assess.

I accept the following benchmark structure:

- **Component checks:** specified type-I, II, and III seesaws; singlet-scalar DM; inert-doublet DM. Each checks supported observables and has an explicit expected outcome for missing targets.
- **Joint checks:** a two-singlet scotogenic implementation plus one inert scalar doublet, and an appropriate seesaw plus an independent Z2-odd scalar within the three-copy budget. The latter is a valid physical baseline with weaker explanatory connections, rather than an automatic scientific failure.
- **Scope checks:** νMSM dark-matter production and Zee–Babu neutrino masses should trigger the relevant unsupported-mechanism status in this evaluator. A quintuplet is outside the proposed representation list and therefore belongs in an external component or scope test. Its mass is a parameter before the thermal-abundance requirement is imposed; it is not generically parameter-free. [Minimal Dark Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0512090) provides the original construction, with later calculations needed for precision benchmarks.
- **Deliberate failures:** anomalous chiral spectra, insufficient neutrino-mass rank, and unstable or necessarily charged putative DM. A failure must identify the actual inconsistency rather than just note that the proposed field name is unfamiliar.

For the tool stack, I agree to name the construction of the most general allowed renormalizable Lagrangian explicitly. We should test available implementations and select supported interfaces. Heavy-state EFT matching can be an efficient branch, but it cannot universally replace full-model calculations for light new states or processes near their production thresholds. Neither a tool's availability nor the existence of an EFT matching result establishes complete precision or collider coverage.

Finally, I agree that Phase 1 should be presented as a reliability and capability study with likely substantial rediscovery. I would correct “novelty enters at three fields”: [Restrepo, Zapata, and Yaguna](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.3655) already considered models with up to four scalar/fermion fields in their classification. Their field-count convention and detailed domain must be compared with ours before claiming coverage or novelty. A new combined constraint analysis, correlation, or verified search improvement can be valuable even when every discrete model is known.

My proposed convergence is now fairly concrete: your bounded enumeration and cost-ordered funnel, our shared-parameter acceptance criterion, explicit evidence levels, a fixed thermal pilot, and a scheduling policy that leaves scientific assessment multidimensional. If you agree to this grammar and the distinction between enumeration and evaluation, I can assemble a shared vision for both of us to review. Numerical ranges, software versions, and runtime budgets should be named implementation decisions to settle by calibration, rather than implicit promises in the vision.
