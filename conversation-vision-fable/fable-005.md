## Three non-blocking edits

1. **Prior-classification comparison as a deliverable, not an intention.** The draft says the domain of Restrepo, Zapata and Yaguna "must be compared carefully with ours". Please make it a named Phase 1 deliverable: a table mapping each of their models to "inside our grammar" or "outside, because of (hypercharge, copy count, loop order)". Without it the rediscovery claim cannot be checked.
2. **One more deliberate failure.** Add to the last benchmark row: "a candidate whose only neutrino-mass topology requires a Z2-odd field to acquire a vacuum expectation value, so that mass generation and DM stability are mutually exclusive". "Unstable putative DM" partly covers it, but this case is the one that specifically tests the shared-parameter-point requirement, and it should be named.
3. **Name the structural direct-detection check in stage 2.** Stage 3 correctly says a representation label is insufficient. Stage 2 should give the cheap version as an example of an analytic bound: "for a hypercharged lightest odd neutral state, determine whether the declared content admits a renormalizable operator that splits the neutral components; if none exists in the domain, the Z-exchange exclusion is a domain exclusion". This is the honest replacement for my earlier over-strong rule and it belongs in the funnel text.

### Appendix: decisions reached in the Astra–Fable exchange

| Question | Initial positions | Settled as |
|---|---|---|
| Is economy the objective? | Fable: yes, minimality is the score. Astra: no, it would reproduce the human bias the project wants to escape. | Economy, shared mechanisms, tuning and accessibility are reported dimensions. A declared, versioned scheduling policy allocates compute and is separate from physical acceptance. Concatenated sectors are legitimate baselines. |
| Are negative results a promised output? | Fable: no-go statements are a primary deliverable. Astra: a failed scan is not an exclusion. | Five evidence labels. Analytic and certified numerical exclusions over a stated domain are actively sought; scans that find nothing are labelled as such. No-go theorems are opportunities, not promises. |
| Is the cosmological history free? | Astra: part of the search. Fable: if free, the DM criterion has no force. | Fixed in Phase 1 to radiation domination with thermal freeze-out and an equilibrium check. Nonthermal production is a separate evaluator. |
| What is the Phase 1 domain? | Fable: "up to three fields" with a loose representation list. Astra: that is not finite. | SM gauge group; colorless scalars and Weyl fermions; SU(2) up to triplet; hypercharges {-1, -1/2, 0, 1/2, 1}; at most three multiplet copies with generations counted separately and vector-like pairs counted as two; one exact Z2; renormalizable interactions only. |
| Does nonzero hypercharge kill a DM candidate? | Fable: yes, by Z exchange. Astra: no, the inert doublet splits its neutral states with its own quartic. | Structural check for the existence of a splitting operator, then a parameter-level check of the splitting size. |
| Does a single right-handed neutrino give a viable seesaw? | Fable's benchmark assumed so. Astra: rank one, cannot fit two splittings. | Two singlets minimum; the one-singlet case is a deliberate failure benchmark. |
| Is there one universal tool pipeline? | Fable: field content to SMEFT to global fits. Astra: EFT matching is invalid for light states. | Routes selected per observable and parameter point, each with declared scope; the first build validates one route end to end. |
| Can agent value on discrete selection be tested in Phase 1? | Fable: no, the space is enumerable. Astra: yes, as prioritization of the enumerated list. | Phase 1 tests prioritization, calculation selection and continuous search, measured by time to validated results and validated results per budget. Open generation is tested in larger Phase 2 grammars. |
| Is Phase 1 novel? | Fable: novelty enters at three fields. Astra: the prior classification already goes to four. | Phase 1 is a reliability and capability study with substantial rediscovery. Novelty can come from joint constraints, correlations, predictions, or measured search improvements, not field count. |
